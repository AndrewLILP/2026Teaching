Good News? Data · demo double lesson

Is the world really ending — or is it the chart?

Students survey their own class, protect each other’s privacy while they do it, then test whether a chart can tell the truth or twist it. Built around a 30-minute YouTube video, of which students watch about five minutes.

SMOKING HAS COLLAPSED! Adult tobacco use fell from 26.2% to 19.5% (2010–2024) Same data, two framings. By the end of Lesson 1, students can explain which one to trust and why.

The lessons

Each lesson page doubles as the slide deck: press P (or the Present button) and click through with the arrow keys or a presentation clicker.

Lesson 1 · I do → We do → You do

Is the world really ending?

Anonymous class survey, data vs information, PII, a live chart of the class’s answers, then four displays of the same data to evaluate.

  • Video clips 01:36–04:52 and 08:09–09:55
  • Students fix a misleading chart built from real WHO figures
 Lesson 2 · Students create

Trends, outliers and your own chart

Verify a hand-copied dataset, validate the class’s hours data and find outliers, then design a chart and pass it on for peer feedback.

  • Three datasets: class survey, class hours, UN child mortality
  • Two rounds of checklist feedback, then one revision
