Lesson 2 of 2 · Enterprise information systems: Analysing data

Trends, outliers and your own chart

Last lesson you judged other people’s charts. Today you check a dataset before trusting it, find the outliers in our own class data, and then design a chart of your own that a classmate will test against the checklist.

CT5-DAT-02 CT5-DPM-01 CT5-COM-01 CT5-SAF-01

Learning intentions

We are learning to:

  • verify and validate data before drawing conclusions from it
  • use a visualisation to reveal a trend or an outlier
  • improve a design using peer feedback

Success criteria

I can:

  • find and correct an error in a dataset by checking it against the source
  • explain why an outlier should be checked before it is removed
  • design a chart that meets the visualisation checklist and shows a trend or an outlier
  • give specific feedback using the checklist, and make one improvement from the feedback I receive

Worked example 6 min I do

Child deaths spiked in 2005… or did they?

A student copied the world’s under-five mortality figures from a website into a spreadsheet by hand. Here is the chart they made. Booklet p. 6 · Dataset A

Think aloud

  1. Does it fit? Every other value is between 37 and 94. A rate of 623 deaths per 1,000 births would mean more than half of all children died: impossible.
  2. Check the source. The original value for 2005 is 62.3. The decimal point was missed when copying.
  3. Fix the data, not the chart. Correct the value, record what you changed and why, then redraw.
  4. Read the trend. Now the real story appears: a steady fall, getting slower after 2015.

Verification

Checking that data matches its source and is correct, for example comparing each copied value with the original.

Validation

Checking that data is sensible and allowed when it is entered, for example “hours must be a number from 0 to 24”.

Collect and process 7 min We do

Our hours data: what gets in, and what stands out?

  1. Take the slip you held last lesson (it has been shuffled again).
  2. Going around the room, read out the answer to question F exactly as it is written, even if it looks wrong.
  3. The teacher types every value. Then we validate, plot and look for outliers.
  • Which entries did validation reject, and which rule caught each one?
  • An outlier was accepted by validation. Is it an error, or could it be real? What would you need to find out?
  • The mean and the median are different. Which one better describes a typical student, and why?
  • Why is reading out shuffled slips safer than everyone saying their own number?

Your task 14 min You do

Design a chart that tells the truth

Plan first, then draw on the grid. Your chart must show a trend or an outlier, and have a headline that is information backed by the data. Booklet pp. 6–7

Foundation · D–E

One topic, one bar chart

Choose one topic from Dataset C. Draw three bars (Better, Same, Worse) starting at zero. Finish the headline: “In our class, most students think ___.”

Core · C

Show the trend or the outlier

Draw Dataset A (corrected) as a line graph, or Dataset B as a dot plot. Label the trend or the outlier on the chart itself, and write an information headline.

Extension · A–B

Tell the whole story

Draw Dataset A and calculate the average fall per year for 1990–2015 and 2015–2024. Mark where progress slowed. Write a headline that is honest about both the good news and the slowdown.

Peer feedback 10 min We do

Pass it on: two rounds of feedback

  1. Pass your booklet, open at your chart, two people clockwise.
  2. Test the chart against the seven checklist items. Tick ✓ or cross ✗ each one on a feedback slip. Booklet p. 8
  3. Write two stars (what works, with a checklist number) and one wish (one specific change).
  4. After 4 minutes, pass it two more places and repeat on the second slip.

Good feedback is…

Kind (about the chart, not the person), specific (“the y-axis has no units”, not “make it better”) and helpful (the author knows exactly what to change).

Iterate 5 min You do

Act on one wish

Read both feedback slips. Pick the wish that would improve your chart the most and make that change now, in a different colour pen so the change is visible.

Then record it: “I changed ___ because my reviewer noticed ___. Now my chart ___.”

How this lesson is graded
