Keep this handy · the visualisation checklist

  1. Honest headline: the title says what the chart shows without exaggerating.
  2. Labels and source: axes, units and where the data came from are shown.
  3. Bars start at zero: otherwise small differences look huge.
  4. Right chart type: bars compare categories, lines show change over time, dot plots show spread.
  5. Nothing cherry-picked: all the relevant data is shown, not just the part that suits a story.
  6. Not colour alone: labels or a legend explain what each colour means.
  7. Sample size shown: the number of responses (n) is given so readers can judge how far to trust it.
