Teacher notes · not for students

Reef Data: three demo lessons

Three 55-minute lessons for Stage 5 Enterprise information systems: Analysing data. Lesson 1 is the observed demo: paper only, strict I do → We do → You do. Lessons 2 and 3 take the same river data through a spreadsheet, then a relational database. Each lesson stands alone. Each page is also the slide deck: press P.

LessonBig ideaStudents produce
1 · Is the reef recovering?Reef data comes from samples collected by people, so it varies and must be verified. A trend can look fine while the swings get bigger.A quadrat estimate, survey data, a hand-plotted chart of the southern reef, an exit ticket
2 · Runoff in a spreadsheetFilter, look up and group a large flat file to compare decades. A blank is not a zero.A one-site report with averages, a chart and an IF decision
3 · Runoff in a databaseRelated tables store each fact once. SQL selects, groups, joins and repairs data.A set of working queries answering report questions, and a tool comparison

Before the lessons

  • Print: one student booklet per student. Print and cut one survey slip per student (Lesson 1). Print the booklet in colour if you can: Quadrat A (page 3) relies on its patterns, but those are also distinguishable in greyscale.
  • Gather: an envelope, mini whiteboards and markers, pencils and rulers.
  • Test the pages on the teaching computer. Lesson 1: press P, then Load sample data on each widget, then Clear. Lesson 3: click Run query on the first box. The database loads in about a second, offline.
  • Lesson 2 files: share reef-runoff.xlsx (or the two CSVs for Google Sheets). XLOOKUP needs Excel 365 or Google Sheets; older Excel uses VLOOKUP (both are on the page).
  • Lesson 3: students can work entirely in the browser page. If your school has DB Browser for SQLite, reef.db opens there too.
  • Keep teacher files private: reef-runoff-solution.xlsx and reef-answers.sql.
  • Queue the video (youtube.com/watch?v=8f6jHtS5B00) and preview both clips.

About the data

Coral figures are real (AIMS annual reports). River sites and trend rates are real (James et al. 2026). The 1,842 river samples are simulated to follow those trends, and the pages and files say so. Planted errors: one in the spreadsheet, three in the database.

Learning intentions and success criteria

Lesson 1 · We are learning to

  • explain how scientists collect reef data, and why they verify it
  • use a sample to estimate something we can’t measure completely
  • describe a trend that includes big swings
  • judge whether a data display informs or misleads

Lesson 1 · I can

  • estimate coral cover from a quadrat using a point count
  • explain why two surveyors can get different answers, and how scientists deal with it
  • describe the coral trend for a region using the size of its rises and falls as evidence
  • use the visualisation checklist to explain whether a chart shows data fairly

Lesson 2 · We are learning to

  • filter and sort a large dataset to find what matters
  • use a lookup function to combine two tables
  • group and aggregate data to compare periods
  • report a finding that is backed by the data

Lesson 2 · I can

  • find and fix an impossible value using sort and filter
  • explain why a blank cell is not the same as zero
  • fill a column with XLOOKUP (or VLOOKUP) from another sheet
  • use AVERAGEIFS or a pivot table to compare a site’s first and last 10 years

Lesson 3 · We are learning to

  • model data as related tables linked by keys
  • write SQL queries to select, group and join data
  • validate and correct data with queries
  • compare a spreadsheet and a database for the same job

Lesson 3 · I can

  • identify the primary key and foreign key that link two tables
  • write a query using WHERE, ORDER BY and GROUP BY
  • join two tables to answer a question neither table answers alone
  • find a duplicate, an impossible value and a missing value, and fix them with UPDATE or DELETE

Answers

Lesson 1

  • Clip: six bleaching events in less than a decade; heavy rain cooled the water; scientists warn that wider swings between damage and recovery weaken the reef.
  • Quadrat A: live coral under points 3, 7, 10, 12, 14, 18, 20, 21 and 24 = 9 points = 36%. Point 17 sits on dead coral right beside live coral, a likely source of disagreement. Quadrat B (Extension, booklet p. 5): points 1, 4, 6–15, 18 and 19 = 14 points = 56%.
  • Sample quadrat data (24): median 36, outliers 9 (wrote the point count, not the %) and 64 (likely counted dead coral as live).
  • Sample survey (24): Q1 most said “Lower” (11), but the data says higher (11% → 35.1%). Q2 rejects “half” (type) and 150 (range). Median guess 20% vs 35.1% actual; outlier 70.
  • MCQs: Q1 B · Q2 D · Q3 A · Q4 C.
  • Displays: A fair (gaps left as gaps). B cherry-picks 2017 vs 2024 in one region, with no source. C has an axis starting at 29%, so 2025 looks about 11 times shorter (real fall: 39.5 → 30.0). D is a pie of three separate percentages that sum to 93.1%, which is meaningless.

  • Core: biggest rise +15 (2020 → 2021); biggest fall −12.2 (2024 → 2025). In 2026, 26.4% is below the long-term average of 29.3%.
  • Extension: average size of the yearly change, ignoring signs = 46.8 ÷ 9 ≈ 5.2 points a year. That is similar, but the North’s recent swings are the largest in its record. Gaps (2018, 2020) mean some changes can’t be calculated, a good discussion point.

Lesson 2

  • Planted error: Samples row 969 (Herbert, 12 Feb 2003), DIN = 999. Clear it and log it.
  • Counts: Burdekin 222 samples; 132 blank SS cells.
  • MCQs: Q1 C · Q2 A · Q3 D.
  • All sites, SS first vs last 10 years (from the solution workbook, recalculated): NJ −37% · SJ +8% · Tully Gorge −76% · Euramo +8% · Herbert −50% · Barratta −33% · Burdekin −56% · Pioneer −49% · Fitzroy −12%. DIN: Barratta +226% (the exception); all others fall or are flat (Fitzroy +2.5%).
  • The simulated samples were built to follow the published trends, so student results should agree in direction with the study’s table. The two “not improving” SS sites (South Johnstone, Euramo) match the study’s “not significant” and “weak increase”.

Lesson 3

  • Planted problems: sample 1595 (Pioneer, 18 Aug 2015) SS = −42 → set to NULL; sample 796 (Euramo, 20 May 2010) DIN is NULL → keep; samples 1502 and 1843 are a duplicate (Burdekin, 12 Feb 2019) → delete 1843. After the fixes there are 1,842 samples.
  • Key results: query 6 = 475.6; the join shows Fitzroy highest SS (449.2). Swapping to DIN puts South Johnstone highest (0.332).
  • Report answers: see reef-answers.sql (Herbert; 114 Pioneer samples; max SS 1,762.8 at Fitzroy on 10 March 1996; and so on).
  • MCQs: Q1 B · Q2 D · Q3 A.

Likely misconceptions

Students may think…Correct it with…
A healthy reef is 100% coral.The Q2 guesses and the info box: there’s always sand and rubble; above 30% is “high”.
Scientific data is exact.Quadrat A: same photo, different answers. AIMS publishes uncertainty ranges and revises past years.
Recovery means the problem is solved.The swings: a record high was followed by the largest one-year fall.
A missing year can be drawn as a straight join.The gaps in the coral chart: joining them invents data.
Blank or NULL is the same as zero.Lesson 2 Q1 and the Lesson 3 NULL query: zero is a measurement, NULL means none was taken.
A bigger % decline means less pollution now.Lesson 2 Q3: rates are relative to each site’s own start.
A database is just a spreadsheet with a different look.Keys, one fact in one place, constraints and saved queries (the comparison table).

Differentiation and grading

Tasks are tiered Foundation (D–E), Core (C) and Extension (A–B), matched to the Stage 5 course performance descriptors. Each lesson page shows the relevant grade strip: computational, design and systems thinking, plus communicating information.

  • Literacy: sentence starters on all writing; vocabulary is hand-written; OpenDyslexic is available from the font toggle.
  • Numeracy: the point count uses ×4; the paper task uses subtraction; Extension compares mean absolute change.
  • SQL support: every query box starts with a working example, so Foundation students edit rather than write from scratch.

For observing colleagues

  • Students generate real data: 24 surveyors, one photo, a spread of answers. Verification becomes necessary, not just something they are told about.
  • Honest data: real coral figures with their revisions and gaps; real site trends; simulated samples clearly labelled.
  • Progression across tools: the same question (is the water cleaner?) is answered in a spreadsheet, then in a database, so students can judge each tool on evidence.
  • Check-for-understanding moments: the sort hinge, the quadrat dot plot, finger-voting on checklist items, and the MCQ hinges marked in each run sheet.

Syllabus coverage

  • Differentiate between data and information (L1)
  • Describe inputs, storage, transmission, processes and outputs in data analysis (L1)
  • Explore the applications of small and big datasets (L1, L3)
  • Collect and interpret data (L1 quadrat and survey)
  • Represent and store data, including appropriate data types and structuring data systematically (L2, L3)
  • Model entities and their attributes using structured data; model relationships using relational data (L3)
  • Compare the usability of a spreadsheet and a database to analyse the same dataset (L2 → L3)
  • Analyse data in both a flat-file and a relational database using queries (L2, L3)
  • Use appropriate methods to collect, store, validate and verify data (all)
  • Summarise data using formulas, functions, aggregate functions and lookup functions (L2)
  • Filter, group and sort data, including conditional formatting and grouping and aggregating (L2)
  • Load, insert and update data in a database (L3)
  • Generate a data visualisation to identify trends and outliers (L1, L2)
  • Perform verification of datasets, calculations and outputs (all)
  • Evaluate peers’ projects using predetermined requirements (L2)

Sources

Values are the headline figures for each year as first reported (2023 values are the previous-year figures given in the 2024 release). The 2026 report covers 121 reefs; long-term averages are from the 2026 report.
  • River sites and trends: James, C.S. et al. (2026), “Water quality trends in river catchments draining to the Great Barrier Reef: an analysis of ~40 years of data”, Journal of Environmental Management; CSIRO summary article (August 2026).
  • Fertiliser use and sugarcane yields: Lewis, S.E. et al. (2026), “Trends in fertiliser use and efficiency in the Great Barrier Reef catchment area”, Journal of Environmental Management.
  • Simulated samples: generated for these lessons to follow each site’s published trend rate. Not real measurements.
