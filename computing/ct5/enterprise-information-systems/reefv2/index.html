Stage 5 · EIS: Analysing data

Reef Data · three demo lessons

Is the Great Barrier Reef recovering?

Forty years of coral surveys and nearly forty years of river monitoring. Students survey a reef photo themselves, read a decade of coral data full of swings and gaps, then take the river runoff data through a spreadsheet and into a relational database.

2017 · northern coral 11% 2024 · record high 39.5% 2025 · biggest one-year fall: 30.0% 2026 · recovering: 35.1% Up, down, up. Scientists warn that bigger swings, not just low years, weaken the reef.
Stage 5 · Year 9 or 10 3 × 55 min, each stands alone CT5-DAT-01 · DAT-02 · THI-01 · COM-01 · DPM-01 Paper → spreadsheet → database

The lessons

Each lesson page doubles as the slide deck: press P (or the Present button) and click through with the arrow keys or a presentation clicker.

Lesson 1 · I do → We do → You do · paper

Is the reef recovering?

Students survey a reef quadrat and see why surveyors disagree. A class perception survey (with sample data for a class of 24), 10 years of AIMS coral data, and four displays to evaluate.

  • Clip 02:13–03:37
  • Paper task: the southern reef’s swings
 Lesson 2 · spreadsheet

Runoff in a spreadsheet

Nine real monitoring sites, 1,842 sample rows. Find an error code, sort and filter, XLOOKUP the land use, compare first and last decades with AVERAGEIFS or a pivot table.

  • Clip 03:37–about 04:50
  • Site report with IF decision
 Lesson 3 · database

Runoff in a database

The same data as two related tables. Students write SQL in the browser: SELECT, WHERE, GROUP BY and JOIN. They find and fix three planted data problems, then compare a spreadsheet with a database.

  • Runs offline in the browser
  • reef.db also opens in DB Browser for SQLite
