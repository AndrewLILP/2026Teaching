Lesson 2 of 3 · Enterprise information systems: Analysing data

Runoff in a spreadsheet

Ocean heat is a global problem, but soil and fertiliser washing off farms is something Australia can fix at home. Scientists studied nine river monitoring sites for up to 37 years. Today you work with their kind of data: almost 2,000 rows of water samples. You will check it, sort and filter it, look up details from a second table, and group it to answer one question: is the water getting cleaner?

Learning intentions

  • represent and store data systematically, understanding data types and their limitations

Success criteria

I can:

  • validate a dataset by finding and correcting an impossible value, and explain why a blank is not zero

Worked example 3 min I do

The real study: nine sites, two measures

Researchers from James Cook University and CSIRO analysed water samples from nine sites. They measured two things:

DIN: fertiliser nitrogen

Dissolved inorganic nitrogen, mostly from fertiliser. It feeds algae that compete with coral.

SS: soil

Suspended sediment. It clouds the water and can smother seagrass and inshore coral.

Read it: 7 of 9 sites show falling DIN, and 7 of 9 show falling sediment. But not all: Barratta Creek’s nitrogen is rising. Good news with an exception is still honest news.
Worked example 4 min I do

Open the data and check it first

Open reef-runoff.xlsx, or import the two CSVs into Google Sheets. Booklet p. 6

Honest about the data

The Sites sheet is real. The Samples sheet is simulated: made for this lesson to follow each site’s published trend, because the real measurements aren’t published in this form. It says so on the Notes sheet.

  1. Structure. Samples has one row per water sample: 1,842 rows. Sites has one row per site: 9 rows. The site_id column links them.
  2. Data types. Dates are dates, concentrations are decimals, IDs are text. Freeze the top row so the headings stay visible.
  3. Blanks. Some SS cells are empty. Those sites hadn’t started measuring sediment yet. A blank means “not measured”, not zero. Typing 0 would drag every average down.
  4. Impossible values. Sort column G (DIN) largest to smallest. The top value is 999, about 1,000 times higher than anything else. That’s an error code, not a measurement. Clear the cell and record it on the Notes sheet.
Practise 5 min We do

Sort, filter and count

  1. Turn on Filter (Data → Filter). Filter site_id to BUR-INK (Burdekin).
  2. Sort SS largest to smallest. Which month do the biggest values fall in? Why? (Hint: the wet season.)
  3. Clear the filter. In a spare cell, count Burdekin’s samples: =COUNTIF(B:B,"BUR-INK")
  4. Count the blank sediment cells: =COUNTBLANK(H2:H1843)

Burdekin has 222 samples. There are 132 blank SS cells. The biggest sediment values are in January to March, when wet-season rain washes soil into rivers.

Lookup functions 5 min We do

Look it up: bring land use into the Samples sheet

Column I (land_use) is empty. The land use is stored once, on the Sites sheet. A lookup finds it using site_id, so it never has to be retyped.

In I2Formula
Excel 365 or Google Sheets=XLOOKUP(B2,Sites!A:A,Sites!D:D)
Older Excel=VLOOKUP(B2,Sites!A:D,4,FALSE)

Fill it down to the last row (double-click the fill handle). Then filter column I for land uses that include “sugarcane”.

Why this matters

Storing land use once and looking it up means a correction made on the Sites sheet updates every sample. Typing it into 1,842 rows risks 1,842 chances for a typo. That idea becomes the heart of a database in Lesson 3.

Group and aggregate 6 min I do We do

First 10 years vs last 10 years

A fair way to see change is to compare the average of a site’s first 10 years with its last 10 years. For Burdekin sediment (monitored 1996–2024):

CellFormulaResult
K2: first 10 years=AVERAGEIFS(H:H,B:B,"BUR-INK",F:F,"<=2005")about 476 mg/L
K3: last 10 years=AVERAGEIFS(H:H,B:B,"BUR-INK",F:F,">=2015")about 210 mg/L
K4: change=(K3-K2)/K2about −56%

Or use a pivot table: Insert → PivotTable. Put site_id in Rows, year in Columns, and Average of SS in Values. It groups 1,842 rows into one average per site per year.

Check your answers with the explorer. It uses the same data, already grouped by year.

Your task 12 min You do

Your site report: is this river getting cleaner?

Choose one site (not Burdekin). Build a one-screen report on a new sheet. Booklet p. 7

A decision cell=IF(K4<0,"Improving","Not improving")

DIN and SS, the chart and the IF cell. Does your result agree with the published trend?

Explain the exception

How this lesson is graded

Evidence sits against developing computing solutions using computational, design and systems thinking (CT5-THI-01) and selecting data, media and processes to communicate information (CT5-DAT-02, CT5-COM-01).

A

“Develops highly effective computing solutions…” and “selects relevant data, media and processes to effectively communicate information”. Today: your report is accurate and complete, explains an exception, and distinguishes pattern from cause.

B

“Develops effective computing solutions…” and “selects relevant data, media and processes to communicate appropriate information”. Today: correct lookups and averages for both measures, a labelled chart and a supported headline.

C

“Develops sound computing solutions…” and “selects appropriate data, media and processes to communicate information”. Today: you fix the error, use a lookup, and compare the first and last 10 years for one measure.

D

“Develops basic computing solutions…” and “uses data to communicate basic information”. Today: you sort and filter to find values for one site.

E

“Uses data to communicate information in a very limited way”. Today: with support, you filter the data to one site. The thinking strand has no Grade E descriptor, so only the communication strand is quoted. “Today” lines describe evidence for this lesson only.
