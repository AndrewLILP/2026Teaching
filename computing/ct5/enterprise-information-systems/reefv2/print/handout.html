Keep this handy · the visualisation checklist

  1. Honest headline: the title says what the chart shows without exaggerating.
  2. Labels and source: axes, units and where the data came from are shown.
  3. Honest scale: bars start at zero.
  4. Right chart type: lines for change over time, bars for comparing amounts, pies only for parts of one whole.
  5. Nothing cherry-picked: all relevant years and regions are shown.
  6. Not colour alone: labels or a legend explain what each colour means.
  7. Gaps and sample shown: missing data isn’t joined up; readers can tell how much data is behind it.

Honest about the data

Coral figures are real (Australian Institute of Marine Science). River site details and trends are real (James et al. 2026). The river samples in Lessons 2 and 3 are simulated to follow those real trends.
