Lesson 2 of 2 · Enterprise information systems: Analysing data

Build a solar dashboard

Last lesson we predicted on paper. Today the spreadsheet does the arithmetic. You will check a dataset for errors, write formulas that calculate growth, predict 2030 two ways, make a decision with an IF formula, and present it all on a one-screen dashboard that a classmate will test.

CT5-DAT-02 CT5-THI-01 CT5-COM-01 CT5-DPM-01

Learning intentions

We are learning to:

  • verify a dataset before using it
  • use spreadsheet formulas and functions to summarise data
  • make predictions and decisions with a spreadsheet
  • present information on a dashboard

Success criteria

I can:

  • find and fix an error by checking units and the source
  • write a growth formula and fill it down a column
  • predict 2030 with FORECAST, and make a decision with IF
  • build a dashboard with a chart, three key numbers and a headline, then improve it from feedback
Warm-up 3 min

“Nearly tripling”: check the maths

06:52 – 07:27 Uzbekistan’s preschoolsEnrolment rose from 27% of young children in 2017 to 75% in 2025. Play clip ↗

On your mini whiteboard:

  1. By how many percentage points did enrolment rise?
  2. By what percentage did it rise? (Hint: increase ÷ starting value.)
  3. Is “nearly tripling” a fair description?

1. 75 − 27 = 48 percentage points.

2. 48 ÷ 27 ≈ 1.78, a 178% increase. As a ratio, 75 ÷ 27 ≈ 2.8 times the starting value.

3. Yes: 2.8 times is “nearly tripling”. That is the same formula we will type into a spreadsheet today: =(new − old) / old.

Evaluate sources 3 min I do

3 terawatts or 2.4? Which number is right?

Last lesson the video said the world passed 3 TW, but our dataset said 2.4 TW at the end of 2025. Here is what three respected sources report:

SourceWorld solar capacityWhen
IRENA (our dataset)about 2,378 GWend of 2025
IEA Photovoltaic Power Systems Programmeabout 2,974 GWend of 2025
SolarPower Europemore than 3,000 GWearly 2026

Why they differ

  • Definitions: sources can count different things, such as only panels connected to the grid, or the panels’ own rating rather than what reaches the grid.
  • Timing: “end of 2025” and “early 2026” are different moments, and solar is growing fast.
  • Estimates: some countries report late, so recent years are partly estimated and revised later.

The rule: record the source and definition with your data, and never mix sources in one chart without saying so.

Worked example 6 min I do

Open the data and check it before you trust it

Open solar-data.xlsx (or import solar-australia.csv into Google Sheets). Watch first, then copy. Booklet p. 6

  1. Look at the structure. One row per year. Column A is the Year (a whole number) and column B is Capacity (GW) (a decimal). One kind of thing in each column.
  2. Check the data types. Numbers line up on the right; text lines up on the left. Anything on the left in column B is text and won’t calculate.
  3. Scan for values that don’t fit. 2015 is 5.033 and 2017 is 7.238, but 2016 says 5878. That is about 1,000 times too big.
  4. Verify against the source. The original is 5.878 GW. Someone typed megawatts instead of gigawatts (1 GW = 1,000 MW).
  5. Fix it and record it. Change B18 to 5.878, then write the change on the Notes sheet: what, why and the source. This is your change log.
Formulas 6 min I do We do

Let the spreadsheet calculate growth

  1. In C3 type =B3-B2. This is the increase from 2000 to 2001.
  2. In D3 type =(B3-B2)/B2, then format it as a percentage. This is the growth rate.
  3. Select C3:D3 and fill down to row 27. The cell references update on each row.
  4. In E3 type =IF(D3>50%,"Boom year","") and fill down. That is a decision formula.
  5. Add conditional formatting to column D: highlight cells greater than 50%.

This is what your sheet should look like once the 2016 error is fixed. Change the threshold and watch the highlighting change.

  • 2009 and 2010 show growth of more than 200%. Are they errors, or real outliers? How could you find out?
  • Since 2019, the increase (column C) has stayed around 4–5 GW, but the growth % (column D) has fallen every year until 2025. How can both be true?
Predict 5 min We do

Predict 2030, then make a decision

CellFormula (Excel and Google Sheets)What it does
H2=B27Latest capacity (2025)
H3=AVERAGE(C22:C27)Average increase per year, 2019–2025 (the increases in rows 22–27)
H4=FORECAST(2030,B21:B27,A21:A27)Straight-line prediction for 2030 from the 2019–2025 trend
H5=(B27/B22)^(1/5)-1Average growth rate per year, 2020–2025
H6=B27*(1+H5)^5Constant-growth prediction for 2030
H7=IF(H4>=60,"On track","Behind")Decision: is the straight-line prediction at least our target of 60 GW? (The target is made up for this task.)

Check your formulas against the explorer. Choose Australia, then switch methods.

Add the predictions

Everything in Foundation, plus the three key numbers with AVERAGE and FORECAST, and the IF decision cell.

Extension · A–B

Compare and justify

Show both predictions side by side. Add the World sheet as a second chart. Write two sentences on which prediction you trust for each, and why. Bonus: doubling time =LN(2)/LN(1+H5).

Peer feedback 7 min We do

Swap seats: test a dashboard

  1. Leave your dashboard on screen. Move two seats clockwise, taking your booklet.
  2. Test the dashboard in front of you against the checklist and the dashboard table. Do not edit it.
  3. On the feedback slip in their booklet, write two stars and one wish, each linked to a checklist number. Tick ✓ or cross ✗ each item.
  4. Check one formula: click a key-number cell. Does the formula do what the label says?

Good feedback is…

Kind, specific (“the y-axis has no units”) and helpful (the author knows exactly what to change).

Iterate 4 min You do

Act on one wish

Go back to your own dashboard. Read the feedback, make the single change that improves it most, then complete the sentence in your booklet: “I changed ___ because my reviewer noticed ___. Now my dashboard ___.”

Save the file as solar-dashboard-yourinitials.xlsx.

“Develops basic computing solutions using computational or design or systems thinking skills” and “uses data to communicate basic information”. Today: you follow the steps to add a growth column and a chart.

E

“Uses data to communicate information in a very limited way”. Today: with support, you insert a chart of the data. The computational, design and systems thinking strand has no Grade E descriptor, so only the communication strand is quoted here. “Today” lines describe evidence for this lesson only.
