Sequence: Drive and Turn

This example uses the Drivetrain on the VR Robot to drive toward a piece of trash, collect it, then turn to face the next piece of trash.

Playground: Coral Reef Cleanup Structure: Sequence No sensors used
CT5-OPL-01 CT5-THI-01 Sequence

Level 1 is the simplest of the four projects: the VR Robot's Drivetrain executes a fixed sequence of instructions with no sensors, no decisions and no repetition. It is the starting point for recognising what a sequence algorithm looks like — every instruction runs exactly once, in the order it is written.

2. Flowchart

3. NSW pseudocode

BEGIN
  DRIVE forward 800 mm
  TURN right 90 degrees
END
Why this matters: a sequence is the simplest of the three control structures (sequence, selection, iteration). Every line here runs once, top to bottom — there is no IF and no REPEAT.

4. Python (VEXcode VR)

The pseudocode above translates directly into VEXcode VR's Python API — each pseudocode line becomes one drivetrain command.

level1_coral_reef_cleanup.py
def main():
    # Drive toward the first piece of trash and collect it.
    drivetrain.drive_for(FORWARD, 800, MM)
    # Turn to face the next piece of trash.
    drivetrain.turn_for(RIGHT, 90, DEGREES)

vr_thread(main())
API notes: drive_for(direction, amount, units) and turn_for(direction, angle, DEGREES) are both waiting commands — the next line does not run until the movement finishes. That's exactly what a sequence needs.

Extension B–A

Q7 — Debug. A student rewrote Level 1 like this. Identify the logic error and explain the effect it would have on the robot's path. (3 marks)

when started
  turn right for 90 degrees
  drive forward for 800 mm
Show answer
The instructions are in the wrong order. The robot now turns 90° before driving, so it collects trash in a completely different location than intended — the sequence order changes the outcome, even though both blocks are individually correct.

Q8 — Extend. Design pseudocode (not VEXcode) for a Level 1b that collects three pieces of trash in a triangle pattern, each 800 mm apart with a 120-degree turn between each. (4 marks)

Show answer
BEGIN
  DRIVE forward 800 mm
  TURN right 120 degrees
  DRIVE forward 800 mm
  TURN right 120 degrees
  DRIVE forward 800 mm
  TURN right 120 degrees
END
Accept answers that note this is still pure sequence — repeating the pattern manually rather than using a loop — and credit responses that additionally suggest a REPEAT 3 TIMES loop as a more efficient alternative (a preview of Level 3's iteration).
