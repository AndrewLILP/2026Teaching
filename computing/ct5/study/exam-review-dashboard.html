Question provenance

Section A (MC) and Section C (extended response) were checked against the source revision material (three study sheets, 95–96 questions each). Section B (short answer) was rebuilt to draw only from terms confirmed in both the official NESA glossary and Andrew's flip-cards. Together this confirms how much of the exam students could reasonably expect to recognise from their own revision.

    Difficulty tier distribution

    Section A (multiple choice) is explicitly tiered per question. Section B (short-answer definitions) sits consistently at Core; Section C (extended response) sits consistently at Extension, reflecting the synthesis and judgement those tasks require.

    Section A — distractor & answer-position analysis

    Two independent checks on the 12 multiple-choice items: whether the correct answer's letter is predictable, and whether its length gives it away regardless of subject knowledge.

    Correct-answer letter distribution

    Exactly even — 3 of each letter across the 12 items. A student guessing "C every time" gains no edge.

    ✓ Length-tell resolved — correct answers now average 6% shorter than distractors

    Correct-answer length dropped from an average of 73.9 characters to 65.0; distractor length rose from 49.5 to 69.2 — an overall ratio of 0.94× (previously 1.49×). All 8 flagged items (Q1, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q12) were rewritten: distractors were lengthened into genuine misconceptions rather than obviously-wrong filler, and correct answers were trimmed to remove unnecessary justification. Letter distribution remains perfectly even (3/3/3/3).

    Findings & recommendations

    Read alongside the strand matrix above — these are the gaps worth a conversation before this paper is used for formal reporting rather than in-class revision.

    ✓ Provenance now 0% new

    Every item now traces to source material — Section A/C to the study sheets, Section B to terms confirmed in both the NESA glossary and the flip-cards. Comfortably inside the ~15% ceiling for a Stage 5 project-based course.

    ✓ Distractor length-tell resolved

    The "always pick the longest option" exploit dropped from 67% to 0% after rewriting the 8 flagged items. Letter distribution remains perfectly even (3/3/3/3).

    ⚠ Algorithms strand still caps at Core

    Now seven CT5-OPL-01 items (pseudocode, desk-checking, tracing, branching, function) sit at Core — more than before, but still none at Extension. No item asks a student to design or modify an algorithm under a novel constraint — the paper still can't discriminate a B/A-level algorithms student from a C-level one.

    ⚠ UI/UX and Automation strands still cap at Core

    Both CT5-DES-01-aligned UI/UX content and the enterprise/innovation/automation strand have Foundation and Core items but nothing at Extension. Unchanged by the Section B rebuild, since all short-answer items sit at Core by design.

    ⚠ Safe/secure/ethical data still has no Foundation item

    A student shaky on the basics has no low-stakes way to show partial understanding on this strand — they clear the Core/Extension items or score zero. Unchanged by the rebuild.

    ✓ CT5-DAT-02 resolved, but CT5-DAT-01 is now the gap

    The Section B rebuild added two compression items (CT5-DAT-02), closing that gap — but the data-validation item that covered CT5-DAT-01 was dropped, since "data validation" wasn't a confirmed glossary term. CT5-DAT-01 is now the one untested outcome. Worth a standalone item if used for outcome-level reporting.

    ℹ CT5-COL-01 and CT5-EVL-01 are CPD "orphans"

    Neither has a dedicated CPD descriptor line — evidence from these items (peer feedback, testing/evaluating) counts diffusely toward the iterative-processes and effective-solutions strands rather than a strand of their own.
