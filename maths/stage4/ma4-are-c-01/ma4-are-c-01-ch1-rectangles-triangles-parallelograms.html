Key vocabulary

Rectangle

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a rectangle, found using A = l × b.
Example
The amount of carpet needed to cover a rectangular room.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a rectangle — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Triangle

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a triangle, found using A = 12 × b × h.
Example
The amount of fabric needed for a triangular flag.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a triangle — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Parallelogram

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a parallelogram, found using A = b × h.
Example
The amount of paint needed to cover a parallelogram-shaped wall panel.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a parallelogram — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Quadrilateral

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside any four-sided shape — found using whichever formula matches that shape, or by splitting it into simpler pieces.
Example
Working out how much turf a four-sided backyard needs.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a quadrilateral — that’s the perimeter, not the area.
I Do — Worked Examples

Choose a level, then reveal the steps one at a time.

Garden bed — a rectangle

Step 1. This is a rectangle, so the formula is A = l × b.
Step 2. Substitute the length and width: A = 6 × 4.
Step 3. A = 24 m².
The garden bed has an area of 24 square metres.

The games room — a composite shape

Step 1. This shape isn’t a single rectangle.
Imagine the full 12 m × 10 m rectangle, then subtract the missing corner.
Step 2. Full rectangle: 12 × 10 = 120 m².
Missing corner: 4 × 5 = 20 m².
Step 3. A = 120 − 20 = 100 m².
The games room needs 100 square metres of flooring.

Roof gable — working backwards, then composite

The triangular gable has an area of 24 cm² and a base of 8 cm. The rectangle below it has the same 8 cm base and a height of 5 cm.

Step 1. Rearrange the triangle formula to find the height: h = 2 × A ÷ b.
Step 2. h = 2 × 24 ÷ 8 = 6 cm. The gable is 6 cm tall.
Step 3. Now find the rectangle’s area: A = l × b = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².
Step 4. Total wall area = 24 + 40 = 64 cm².
The gable (24 cm²) plus the rectangle below it (40 cm²) gives the full wall area.

A triangle is always exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height — which is why the triangle formula has a 12 in it.

Newman’s Problem

A tent’s side wall is shaped like a house — a rectangle with a triangular roof on top.
The rectangle is 4 m wide and 3 m tall.
The triangular section above it has the same 4 m base and is 1.5 m tall.
How much fabric is needed for one side wall?

Stage 1 — Read

Read the problem through carefully before doing anything else.

The question is asking me to find ___

Stage 2 — Comprehend

What do you know from the problem?

I know that the rectangle is 4 m × 3 m, and the triangle has base 4 m and height 1.5 m.

Stage 3 — Transform

What formula or operation do you need?

I need to find the rectangle’s area (A = l×b) and the triangle’s area (A = 12bh), then add them together.

Stage 4 — Process

Work it out.

Rectangle: 4 × 3 = 12 m². Triangle: 12 × 4 × 1.5 = 3 m². Total: 12 + 3 = 15 m².

Stage 5 — Encode

Write your final answer in a full sentence.

Rectangle area = 4 × 3 = 12 m².
Triangle area = 12 × 4 × 1.5 = 3 m².
The tent wall needs 15 m² of fabric.
