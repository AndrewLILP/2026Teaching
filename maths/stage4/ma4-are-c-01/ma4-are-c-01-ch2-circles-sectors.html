Area of circles and sectors

The circular seating area

Your local park has a circular outdoor seating area, paved in brick.
The council wants to know how much paving it covers before they plan any repairs.

How much room is in this circular space?
We’ll need a new formula — one built for curves, not straight sides.

Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it.

Circle

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a circle, found using A = π·r².
Example
The amount of grass covering a circular lawn.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a circle — that’s the circumference, not the area.

Sector

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a “pie-slice” part of a circle, found using A = θ360 × π·r².
Example
How much of a pizza one slice covers.
Non-example
The length of the curved edge of a sector — that’s an arc length, not an area.

Quadrant

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a quarter-circle, found by taking 14 of the full circle’s area.
Example
How much space one quarter of a circular garden bed covers.
Non-example
The perimeter of a quadrant (the two straight edges plus the curved edge) — that’s a length, not an area.

Semicircle

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside half a circle, found by taking half of the full circle’s area.
Example
How much space a semicircular rug covers.
Non-example
The perimeter of a semicircle (the straight diameter plus the curved arc) — that’s a length, not an area.
I Do — Worked Examples

Choose a level, then reveal the steps one at a time.

A circular pizza base

Step 1. This is a full circle, so the formula is A = π·r².
Step 2. Substitute the radius: A = π × 5² = π × 25.
Step 3. A ≈ 78.5 cm² (using π ≈ 3.14).
The pizza base has an area of about 78.5 square centimetres.

The park seating area — a semicircle

This bench seat is only a semicircle — half a circle, so θ = 180°.

Step 1. Use the sector formula: A = (θ⁄360) × π·r².
Step 2. Substitute θ = 180 and r = 6: A = (180⁄360) × π × 6² = 0.5 × π × 36.
Step 3. A ≈ 56.5 m² (using π ≈ 3.14).
The seating area covers about 56.5 square metres.

A stage design — rectangle plus quadrant

The stage floor is a rectangle 8 m by 5 m, with a quarter-circle (radius 5 m) attached to one side.

Step 1. Find the rectangle’s area: A = l × b = 8 × 5 = 40 m².
Step 2. Find the quadrant’s area — a quadrant is 14 of a full circle: A = 14 × π·r² = 14 × π × 5².
Step 3. A ≈ 19.6 m² (using π ≈ 3.14).
Step 4. Total stage area = 40 + 19.6 = 59.6 m².
Add the two separate areas together to get the whole stage.

Newman’s Problem

A garden sprinkler is fixed in place and rotates through 270° as it sprays.
It can reach 4 m in every direction it sprays.
What area of the garden gets watered?

Stage 1 — Read

Read the problem through carefully before doing anything else.

The question is asking me to find ___

Stage 2 — Comprehend

What do you know from the problem?

I know that the sprinkler sprays through 270°, and it reaches 4 m in every direction.

Stage 3 — Transform

What formula or operation do you need?

I need the sector area formula: A = (θ⁄360) × πr², using θ = 270 and r = 4.

Stage 4 — Process

Work it out.

A = (270⁄360) × 3.14 × 4² = 0.75 × 3.14 × 16 ≈ 37.7 m².

Stage 5 — Encode

Write your final answer in a full sentence.

A = (270⁄360) × π × 4² = 0.75 × 3.14 × 16 ≈ 37.7 m².
The sprinkler waters about 37.7 m² of the garden.
