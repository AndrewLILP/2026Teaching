Area of trapeziums, rhombuses and kites

Font

Designing a kite

Your school is holding a kite-flying day.
You’re designing two possible kite frames — a symmetrical rhombus frame, and a traditional asymmetrical kite frame.
Both are held together by two diagonal struts.

How much fabric would each frame need?
Both shapes use their diagonal struts to work out area — even though they look quite different.

Key vocabulary

Tap each card to flip it.

Trapezium

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a trapezium, found using A = h2 × (a + b).
Example
The amount of paving needed for a trapezium-shaped garden bed.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a trapezium — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Rhombus

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a rhombus, found using A = 12 × x × y (x and y are the diagonals).
Example
The amount of fabric needed to cover a rhombus-shaped kite panel.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a rhombus — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Kite

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a kite, found using A = 12 × x × y — the same formula as a rhombus.
Example
The amount of paper needed to make a kite for kite-flying day.
Non-example
The distance around the edge of a kite — that’s the perimeter, not the area.

Composite Figure

Tap to flip
Definition
The amount of space inside a shape made of two or more simpler shapes joined together, found by adding (or subtracting) the area of each part.
Example
The total floor area of an L-shaped room.
Non-example
The perimeter of a composite figure — that’s a length, not an area.
I Do — Worked Examples

Choose a level, then reveal the steps one at a time.

A kite

Step 1. This is a kite, so the formula is A = 12 × x × y (x and y are the diagonals).
Step 2. Substitute the diagonals: A = 12 × 8 × 6.
Step 3. A = 24 cm².
The kite has an area of 24 square centimetres.

A trapezium garden bed

Step 1. Use the trapezium formula: A = h2 × (a + b).
Step 2. Substitute a = 5, b = 9, h = 4: A = 42 × (5 + 9) = 2 × 14.
Step 3. A = 28 m².
The garden bed has an area of 28 square metres.

A kite — working backwards

This kite has an area of 60 cm² and one diagonal of 10 cm. Find the other diagonal.

Step 1. Rearrange the kite formula to find y: y = 2 × A ÷ x.
Step 2. Substitute A = 60 and x = 10: y = 2 × 60 ÷ 10 = 120 ÷ 10.
Step 3. y = 12 cm.
The other diagonal is 12 centimetres long.

Newman’s Problem

A rhombus-shaped road sign has diagonals 60 cm and 80 cm.
Reflective vinyl to cover it costs $12 per m².
How much does it cost to cover one sign?

Stage 1 — Read

Read the problem through carefully before doing anything else.

The question is asking me to find ___

Stage 2 — Comprehend

What do you know from the problem?

I know the diagonals are 60 cm and 80 cm, and vinyl costs $12 per m².

Stage 3 — Transform

What formula or operation do you need?

I need the rhombus area formula, then I need to convert cm² to m² before I can use the cost rate.

Stage 4 — Process

Work it out.

Area = half of 60 × 80 = 2400 cm² = 0.24 m². Cost = 0.24 × $12 = $2.88.

Stage 5 — Encode

Write your final answer in a full sentence.

Area = half of 60 × 80 = 2400 cm².
2400 cm² = 0.24 m².
Cost = 0.24 × $12 = $2.88.
Covering one sign costs $2.88.
