Key vocabulary

Square centimetre (cm²)

Tap to flip
Definition
A unit for measuring area — the space covered by a square 1 cm by 1 cm.
Example
The area of a postage stamp or a small tile.
Non-example
1 cm — that’s a length, not an area.

Square metre (m²)

Tap to flip
Definition
A unit for measuring area — the space covered by a square 1 m by 1 m.
Example
The floor area of a small bedroom, or a single parking space.
Non-example
1 m — that’s a length, not an area.

Hectare (ha)

Tap to flip
Definition
A unit for measuring large areas of land — equal to 10,000 m² (a square 100 m by 100 m).
Example
The size of a large park, or a small farm paddock.
Non-example
1 km² — a hectare is much smaller than a square kilometre (100 hectares make 1 km²).

Square kilometre (km²)

Tap to flip
Definition
A unit for measuring very large areas — a square 1 km by 1 km, equal to 1,000,000 m².
Example
The size of a suburb, or a large national park.
Non-example
1 ha — a square kilometre is much bigger than a hectare (1 km² = 100 ha).
I Do — Worked Examples

Choose a level, then reveal the steps one at a time.

Converting m² to cm²

Convert 5 m² to cm².

Step 1. Recall: 1 m² = 10,000 cm² (since 1 m = 100 cm, and area scales by 100 × 100).
Step 2. Multiply: 5 × 10,000.
Step 3. 5 m² = 50,000 cm².

Converting a farm to hectares

A farm paddock covers 25,000 m². Convert this to hectares, and decide if hectares is a sensible unit to report it in.

Step 1. Recall: 1 ha = 10,000 m².
Step 2. Divide: 25,000 ÷ 10,000 = 2.5.
Step 3. 25,000 m² = 2.5 ha.
Reporting this as 2.5 ha is far more sensible than 25,000 m² — the number stays small and easy to compare with other farm sizes. In km² it would be an awkwardly small 0.025 km².

A scale floor plan

A floor plan shows a room as 25 mm by 20 mm. The scale is 1 mm on the plan = 100 mm in real life. Find the room’s real area in m².

Step 1. Convert the plan length: 25 mm × 100 = 2500 mm = 2.5 m.
Step 2. Convert the plan width: 20 mm × 100 = 2000 mm = 2 m.
Step 3. Calculate the real area: A = 2.5 × 2 = 5 m².
Convert the linear scale first, then use the real-world lengths to find area — converting the area figure directly by the linear scale factor would be a mistake.

Newman’s Problem

A farmer is reseeding a paddock 250 m by 400 m.
Reseeding costs $45 per hectare.
What is the total cost?

Stage 1 — Read

Read the problem through carefully before doing anything else.

The question is asking me to find ___

Stage 2 — Comprehend

What do you know from the problem?

I know the paddock is 250 m by 400 m, and reseeding costs $45 per hectare.

Stage 3 — Transform

What formula or operation do you need?

I need to find the area in m², convert it to hectares, then multiply by the cost per hectare.

Stage 4 — Process

Work it out.

Area = 250 × 400 = 100,000 m². In hectares: 100,000 ÷ 10,000 = 10 ha. Cost = 10 × $45 = $450.

Stage 5 — Encode

Write your final answer in a full sentence.

Area = 250 × 400 = 100,000 m² = 10 ha.
Cost = 10 × $45 = $450.
Reseeding the whole paddock costs $450.
