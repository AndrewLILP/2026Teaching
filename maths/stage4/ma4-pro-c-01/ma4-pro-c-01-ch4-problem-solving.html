Newman's Analysis: The Raffle (fully worked)

A raffle sells 400 tickets. Maya buys 20.
Question: what is the probability Maya does not win?

Stage 1: Read
"A raffle sells 400 tickets. Maya buys 20. What is the probability Maya does not win?"
Stage 2: Comprehend
We need the complement of winning — the probability Maya's ticket is not drawn.
Stage 3: Transform
P(Maya wins) = 20400 = 120
Stage 4: Process
P(Maya does not win) = 1 − 120 = 1920
Stage 5: Encode
Maya has a 19/20 probability of not winning the raffle.

Resolving the Hook

Both numbers matter — because they're complements, knowing one instantly tells you the other (they always sum to 1). Insurers use the full picture: the claim probability helps estimate expected payout costs, while the no-claim probability tells them how many drivers will pay premiums without costing them anything. Good decisions in probability almost always use both an event and its complement together, not just one number in isolation.

The Birthday Problem

In a classroom of 23 students, what's the probability that at least 2 of them share a birthday?

The probability is approximately 50.7% — more likely than not! This feels surprising, but it's solved using the complement: it's far easier to calculate P(no one shares a birthday) first, then use P(at least one shared birthday) = 1 − P(no shared birthdays). Directly counting every possible way two or more people could share a birthday is extremely complex, but counting the one way nobody shares is much more manageable.

Why is this problem solved by first finding P(no shared birthdays)?

Deal or No Deal

You're playing a game show. 5 boxes remain unopened. One contains $100,000; the other four contain much smaller amounts. The bank offers you $18,000 to stop playing now.

If you continue playing, what is the probability your own box holds the $100,000?

Notice that opening the other boxes doesn't change the probability your own box holds the prize — that stayed fixed at 1/5 the moment boxes were assigned. What changes is the size of the bank's offer, since it's based on the shrinking pool of unknown values still in play.
