Put these in order — without converting anything

Hook

Here are eight numbers, written in different forms:

12, 0.3̇5̇, 75%, 35, 17%, 0.02, 145, 0.8

Try to arrange them from smallest to largest — but here's the catch: don't convert any of them first.
Just look at them as they are and see how far you get.

Tricky, right?
A fraction, a recurring decimal, two percentages, a terminating decimal, and a mixed number all look completely different from each other.
There's no reliable way to compare them at a glance while they're in different forms.
Convert every number into the same form — decimals are usually the easiest for comparing — and then ordering them becomes simple.
That's exactly the skill this chapter builds: moving confidently between fractions, decimals, and percentages, so you can always choose whichever form makes a problem easiest.
Converting everything to decimals: 0.02, 0.17, 0.3̇5̇ ≈ 0.356, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.8, 1.8

So in their original forms, the ascending order is:
0.02, 17%, 0.3̇5̇, 12, 35, 75%, 0.8, 145

Equivalent fractions and simplifying

Equivalent fractions represent the same value, even though they're written with different numbers.
To find the highest common factor (HCF) of 16 and 24, list their factors: the largest number that divides both is 8.
So the HCF of 16 and 24 is 8.

We can show 23 is equivalent to 46 using an area model or a number line — halving each unit doubles the number of units, but the shaded amount stays the same.

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their HCF.
For example, 1216 simplifies to 34, since the HCF of 12 and 16 is 4.

We Do

Simplify it two ways

Simplify 1830 first by finding the HCF directly, then again by repeatedly dividing by common factors (for example, dividing by 2, then by 3).
Do you get the same simplified fraction both times?

HCF method: HCF of 18 and 30 is 6, so 18÷6 = 3 and 30÷6 = 5, giving 35.
Repeated method: 18÷2=9, 30÷2=15, giving 915; then 9÷3=3, 15÷3=5, giving 35.
Both methods give the same simplified fraction — the HCF method just gets there in one step.
I Do

Rational numbers

A rational number is any number that can be written in the form ab, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0.

2 is rational, because it can be written as 21.
47% is rational, because it can be written as 47100.
From Chapter 1, you already know that any number with a terminating or recurring decimal is rational — because it can always be converted back into a fraction of integers, even if that takes a bit of work.

We Do

Spot the one that doesn't belong

Look at this list of numbers: 5, −34, 0.7̇, 60%, and 0.101001000100001...
Four of these are rational. One is not.
Which one, and why?

0.101001000100001... is the odd one out.
Its decimal never terminates and never settles into a repeating block — the number of zeros keeps increasing — so it cannot be written as a fraction of integers.
That makes it irrational, just like √2 and π from Chapter 1.
The other four numbers can all be written as ab, so they're rational.
I Do

Converting between forms

A calculator shows 23 as 0.6666666667 — the display is rounded, but the true value is the recurring decimal 0.6̇.

Going the other way, a terminating decimal converts back to a fraction using place value: 0.9 = 910.
Recurring decimals need a slightly different approach: 0.53̇5̇... converts to 5399, using the number of repeating digits to decide the denominator.

Question 7 · Problem Solving (Newman's Analysis)

Three stores are having sales on the same jacket, originally $80.
Store A takes 14 off.
Store B multiplies the price by 0.7.
Store C takes 22% off.
Which store offers the best deal (the lowest price)?

Stage 2 — Comprehend: What is the problem asking you to find?
Stage 3 — Transform: What will you need to do to compare the three discounts fairly?
Stage 4 — Process: Calculate Store B's price: $80 × 0.7.
Stage 5 — Encode: Now compare all three stores. Which store offers the best deal?
Store A: $80 × (1 − 14) = $80 × 34 = $60
Store B: $80 × 0.7 = $56
Store C: $80 × (1 − 0.22) = $80 × 0.78 = $62.40
Store B offers the lowest price, at $56.

Reflect

Key ideas from this chapter

Equivalent fractions represent the same value; the HCF gives the fastest route to fully simplified form.
Every rational number can be written as ab, including whole numbers, terminating decimals, recurring decimals, and percentages.
Converting between fractions, decimals, and percentages lets you choose whichever form makes a comparison or calculation easiest — usually decimals, when you need to order several values quickly.
