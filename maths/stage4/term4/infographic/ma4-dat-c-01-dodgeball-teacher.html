Today

What we’re learning

Learning intention

We are learning to represent a dataset using a statistical infographic, justify the graph we chose, and describe the data using measures of centre and spread.

Success criteria — I can…

  • match a dataset to the graph type that suits it best, and explain why another would be poor
  • draw a dot plot and a stem-and-leaf plot accurately
  • calculate the mean, median, mode and range
  • explain how an outlier changes the mean and the median

Centre & spread · 5 min

Students work each measure on mini whiteboards before you reveal it.

Median: with an even number of values, the median is halfway between the two middle values — 12.5 is not one of the data values, and that is fine.

Mean: total hits 156 (checked). 156 ÷ 12 = 13.

Range is a measure of spread, not centre.

What about student11?

student11 played only 3 of 8 games

2 hits is far from the rest — an outlier.

Predict first

If we leave student11 out, will the mean go up or down?
Which will change more: the mean or the median?

Mean1314  (154 ÷ 11)

The mean moved more than the median.
An outlier pulls the mean towards it.
The median is a better “typical” value when there is an outlier.

Outlier · 3 min

Ask whether it is fair to leave student11 out. There is no single right answer: if the question is “how many hits does a typical player get over a full season?”, leaving them out (or using the median) is reasonable. If the question is “how did the whole squad go?”, include everyone.

This is the “Think deeper” question on worksheet page 5.

Writing a justification

“Because…” is where the marks are

I chose a dot plot
because the data is numerical
and I want my reader to see the spread and the outlier.
A line graph would be less suitable because the players are not in any order.
Useful words: categorical · numerical · spread · cluster · outlier · median · mode · range · parts of a whole · over time · compare
