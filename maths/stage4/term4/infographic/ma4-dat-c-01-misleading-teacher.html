Today

What we’re learning

Learning intention

We are learning to recognise how a graph can mislead, and to redraw it so it represents the data honestly.

Success criteria — I can…

  • use the six detective questions to check any graph
  • name the trick a misleading graph uses
  • explain what the reader is meant to think, and why that is wrong
  • redraw a misleading graph honestly

Opening · 3 min

Class prayer or reflection as usual. Reveal each criterion.

Hook · from the Kangaroos newsletter

Is this headline true?

THE KANGAROO COURT · SEASON WRAP
KANGAROOS SMASH THE LEAGUE!
“We hit five times more players than the Quokkas!”
On your mini whiteboard:
How many times taller is the Kangaroos column than the Quokkas column?
156 hits vs 139 hits.
That’s 17 more, about 12% more.
Not five times more.

Hook · 5 min

Students estimate the height ratio: about 5 times (21 vs 4 units above 135). Then show the honest graph starting at zero.

Every number in the misleading graph is correct. The trick is the axis starting at 135.

Check: (156 − 139) ÷ 139 ≈ 12.2%.

Your toolkit

Six detective questions

Ask…If not, the trick is…
1. Does the vertical axis start at zero?a truncated axis makes small differences look huge
2. Are pictures scaled fairly?a distorted pictogram: doubling height and width makes the picture 4 times the area
3. Does the scale go up in equal steps?an uneven scale stretches the part the author wants you to notice
4. Is all the data shown?cherry-picking: only the part that tells the story is shown
5. Do the parts add to the whole?a sector graph that doesn’t add to 100%
6. Is there a title, labels, a scale and a source?missing information, so the reader can’t check the claim

Graph Detective · 15 min

For each case: read the claim, students write which detective question fails, then reveal the trick, then the honest version. About 2 minutes per case.

1 Truncated axis (axis starts at 135). 2 Pictogram: Kangaroos’ ball is twice as tall and twice as wide, so 4 times the area for 2 times the catches. 3 Uneven scale: 0, 15, 17, 19, 21 equally spaced. 4 Cherry-picked: only R5–R6 shown; season mean is 3. 5 Poll adds to 110% because fans could choose more than one role, so a sector graph is impossible. 6 No scale or labels: the claim can’t be checked.

Think · pair · share

Every number was correct. Was it still dishonest?

Who might make a misleading graph?
A newsletter writer, a sponsor, a team wanting more funding, an advertiser…
Why does it matter?
People make decisions from graphs: who to pick, what to buy, how to vote.
A graph detective doesn’t just read the numbers.
They check whether the picture tells the same story as the numbers.

We Do · Worksheet Case 2

How much bigger does the Kangaroos ball look?

Kangaroos catches: 60
Possums catches: 30
The ball was drawn 2 times as tall and 2 times as wide.
Small ball: 1 × 1 = 1 square unit
Big ball: 2 × 2 = 4 square units
Twice the catches, but 4 times the area.
Fix: use same-size icons.
1 ball = 10 catches → 6 balls vs 3 balls.

We Do · 5 min

Ask students to estimate first: “How many small balls fit inside the big one?” Use grid squares to show 4. Then toggle to the honest pictogram.
