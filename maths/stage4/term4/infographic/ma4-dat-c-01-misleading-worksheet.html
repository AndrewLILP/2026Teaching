Learning intention: We are learning to recognise how a graph can mislead, and to redraw it so it represents the data honestly.

The six detective questions

Ask…If not, the trick is…
1. Does the vertical axis start at zero?Truncated axis: small differences look huge
2. Are pictures scaled fairly?Distorted pictogram: 2× as tall and 2× as wide = 4× the area
3. Does the scale go up in equal steps?Uneven scale: part of the graph is stretched
4. Is all the data shown?Cherry-picking: only the helpful part is shown
5. Do the parts add to the whole?Doesn’t add to 100%: a sector graph that can’t be right
6. Is there a title, labels, a scale and a source?Missing information: the reader can’t check the claim

Part A · Name the trick

Word bank: truncated axis · distorted pictogram · uneven scale · cherry-picking · doesn’t add to 100% · missing information
GraphTrick
1. A cereal ad shows a bowl drawn twice as tall and twice as wide to show “double the fibre”.
2. Canteen sales rose from 480 to 500 pies. The axis of the column graph starts at 470.
3. A football club’s graph shows only its 3-game winning streak from a 20-game season.
4. A survey shown as a sector graph: 60%, 30%, 25%.
5. The vertical axis is labelled 0, 10, 20, 50, 100, with equal gaps between labels.
6. A graph titled “Sales soar!” has no numbers on its axis and no source.
