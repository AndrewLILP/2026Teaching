Today

What we’re learning

Learning intention

We are learning to represent parts of a whole with sector graphs, divided bar graphs and pictograms, and to compare groups of different sizes using percentages.

Success criteria — I can…

  • convert counts to percentages of a total
  • calculate sector angles and draw an accurate sector graph
  • compare two or more wholes using divided bar graphs
  • explain why percentages give a fairer comparison than counts when totals differ

Percentage = count ÷ total × 100
Always check the parts add to 100%.
If they don’t, something is wrong.

Drawing a sector graph

Percentage → angle

A full circle is 360°.
So 1% of the circle = 360° ÷ 100 = 3.6°.
How out%Angle
Hit6060 × 3.6 = 216°
Caught22.522.5 × 3.6 = 81°
Stepped out17.517.5 × 3.6 = 63°
Total100360° ✓

5 min

Alternative: count ÷ total × 360 (144 ÷ 240 × 360 = 216°). Both methods give the same answer.

The worksheet circle has 100 tick marks, so students without a protractor can count percentage ticks instead.

Fair comparisons

Who gets caught out more: throwers or catchers?

RoleCaught outTotal outsPercentage caught
Throwers (5)3410034%
Catchers (4)88010%
All-rounders (3)126020%
Now hit outs: catchers 60, throwers 48.
Catchers were hit more often. But the groups have different totals.
Catchers: 60 ÷ 80 = 75% of their outs were hits.
Throwers: 48 ÷ 100 = 48%.
When the totals are different, compare percentages, not counts.
Divided bars make this easy to see.

5 min

Ask why throwers get caught out more: they throw more, so more of their throws can be caught.

This is the core reasoning for Panel 2 and Panel 5.

Step 4
Colour, key, title and source.
I chose a sector graph because the three ways out are parts of one whole: 240 outs.
A dot plot would be less suitable because the data is categories, not a list of numbers.

About the data

This season is fictional

The Trinity Red Kangaroos and every statistic were made up for this lesson.
They match the earlier Red Kangaroos lessons: 144 hits against, 156 hits for, 60 catches.
Take it further: survey how your class gets to school, then compare two classes with divided bars.

Reference data

Kangaroos got out: hit 144, caught 54, stepped out 42 (240).
Opponents got out by the Kangaroos: hit 156, caught 60, stepped out 24 (240).
By role (hit, caught, stepped): throwers 48, 34, 18 · catchers 60, 8, 12 · all-rounders 36, 12, 12.
